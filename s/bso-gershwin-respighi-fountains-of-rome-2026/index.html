Thibaudet Plays Gershwin as Yamada Leads Respighi at Symphony Hall

Friday, Nov. 27, at 1:30 p.m. and Saturday, Nov. 28, at 8:00 p.m. at Symphony Hall, 301 Massachusetts Ave., Back Bay.

The Boston Symphony Orchestra's program Gershwin and Respighi: Fountains of Rome brings the French pianist Jean-Yves Thibaudet and the conductor Kazuki Yamada to Symphony Hall for two performances. The first is Friday, Nov. 27, at 1:30 p.m. The second is Saturday, Nov. 28, at 8:00 p.m.

The program

The BSO's listing sets out four works: Maurice Ravel's Pavane for a Dead Princess, George Gershwin's Concerto in F, and two Ottorino Respighi works, Fountains of Rome and Roman Festivals. The page describes the Ravel as the opening piece and calls it introspective.

The soloist and the conductor

The BSO's page describes Thibaudet as the 2026 Avery Fisher Prize winner. It says he brings a "sparkling, jazz-seasoned style" to Gershwin's piano concerto, which blends the composer's jazz and classical sides. Yamada has led the orchestra twice at Tanglewood, according to the page, and makes his Symphony Hall debut leading two of Respighi's colorful homages to Rome.

Both concerts take place in Symphony Hall, which the BSO lists at 301 Massachusetts Avenue in Boston, 02115. The Saturday performance starts at 8:00 p.m., an evening slot for the same program.

Before the concert

On Friday, Nov. 27, the BSO lists a pre-concert talk at 12:15 p.m. by Elizabeth Seitz, ahead of the 1:30 p.m. performance.

Tickets

Single tickets are sold through the buy-tickets link for each performance. The orchestra also offers subscription packages, and the Friday-Fall B subscription of five concerts includes this Friday program. Customer service is at 617-266-1200.
