Young Musicians of the Boston Philharmonic Take On Symphony Hall

Friday, November 6, at 8 PM at Symphony Hall in the Back Bay, with Benjamin Zander conducting Glinka, Stravinsky and Tchaikovsky.

The Boston Philharmonic Youth Orchestra, an ensemble whose players are chosen by audition, takes the stage at Symphony Hall on Friday, November 6, at 8 PM.

The program is an ambitious one for a youth orchestra. Glinka's Ruslan and Ludmila overture opens the evening with a quick, bright burst of energy. Stravinsky's Petrushka follows, a ballet score full of color and rhythm, and the concert closes with Tchaikovsky's Symphony No. 5, about 44 minutes of soaring Romantic writing.

On the podium

Benjamin Zander, who leads the Boston Philharmonic, conducts the concert. It is presented as a visiting event at Symphony Hall, and the program is built to show what the orchestra is all about.

Getting there and tickets

Symphony Hall stands at 301 Massachusetts Avenue in the Back Bay, a short walk from Symphony station on the Green Line. Tickets are sold through the Buy Tickets link on the Symphony Hall listing, and the box office can be reached at 617-266-1200. Prices are not posted on the page we checked, so the box office is the quickest way to ask.

Youth concerts like this are a chance to hear the next generation of players at full strength in one of the country's best-known concert halls. Bring a friend who has never heard Tchaikovsky's Fifth live; it is hard to forget.
