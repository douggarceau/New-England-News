Earl Lee Leads Tchaikovsky's Fourth at Symphony Hall, Nov. 24-25

Franck, Tomasi and Tchaikovsky on one program, with saxophonist Steven Banks, at 1:30 PM on Nov. 24 and 8:00 PM on Nov. 25 at Symphony Hall.

Earl Lee, the Boston Symphony Orchestra's assistant conductor, leads the orchestra at Symphony Hall in a program that moves from French romanticism to Tchaikovsky. The concerts are on Nov. 24 at 1:30 PM and Nov. 25 at 8:00 PM.

The program

The evening opens with Franck's Le Chasseur maudit, which the orchestra translates as The Cursed Hunter. It is based on a ballad about a man who hunts on the Sabbath and is doomed to be chased by demons.

Steven Banks, the saxophone soloist, then plays Tomasi's Concerto for Alto Saxophone and Orchestra. The BSO describes the 1949 work as sultry and atmospheric.

After intermission, the program closes with Tchaikovsky's Symphony No. 4. The orchestra notes that the symphony opens with its 'fate' motif and is known for emotional intensity and melodic beauty.

Before the concert

A preview at 12:15 PM on Nov. 24, given by Robert Kirzinger, the BSO's director of program publications, is included with the ticket. The preview is a good way to hear the program explained before the 1:30 PM performance.

Where and when

Symphony Hall is at 301 Massachusetts Avenue in Boston, MA 02115. The Nov. 24 concert begins at 1:30 PM and the Nov. 25 concert begins at 8:00 PM. The BSO's event page does not list ticket prices, so check the page for current seating options and availability.

The orchestra's listing also warns that MBTA construction is under way near the Symphony station on the Green Line, so allow extra time to arrive. Tickets are available through the BSO's event page.
