The Book of Esther in the Age of Rembrandt at the Gardner

On view through January 18, 2027 in the Hostetter Gallery at the Isabella Stewart Gardner Museum on the Fenway.

Through January 18, 2027, the Hostetter Gallery at the Isabella Stewart Gardner Museum on the Fenway is showing The Book of Esther in the Age of Rembrandt, an exhibition built around one of the Hebrew Bible's most dramatic stories. The show opened in August and was co-organized by the Jewish Museum in New York and the North Carolina Museum of Art in Raleigh.

The story of Esther, a Jewish heroine who saves her people from a plot against them, was widely loved in Rembrandt's Holland. The exhibition brings together Dutch paintings, tapestries, decorative arts and Jewish ceremonial objects that depict or relate to Esther, and it traces how audiences of many faiths found inspiration in her courage across different times and places.

Taking your time

The show suits a slow, unhurried visit. The objects reward close looking as much as the paintings do, and the Gardner's small galleries make it easy to move from a tapestry to a manuscript page and back again. Read the labels for the connections between the Dutch works and the Jewish objects, which is where the exhibition does its most careful work.

Plan your visit

The Isabella Stewart Gardner Museum is at 25 Evans Way in the Fenway. Run dates for the exhibition are listed on the museum's website, along with general hours. Check the museum's pages for admission and any timed-entry details before you go, since those can change.
