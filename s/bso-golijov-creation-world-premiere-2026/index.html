Golijov's Creation: a BSO World Premiere, Nov. 12-14

Eric Jacobsen conducts the new Golijov, Hwang and Cohen work Nov. 12, 13 and 14 at Symphony Hall, 301 Massachusetts Ave.

A new large-scale work by Osvaldo Golijov is an event in itself. When the Boston Symphony Orchestra presents his Creation at Symphony Hall, it will be a world premiere commissioned by the orchestra, and the culminating event of the BSO's Creation festival.

An answer to Haydn

Golijov, the Argentine-born American composer, wrote the music. David Henry Hwang, the Tony Award-winning playwright who wrote the libretto for Golijov's opera Ainadamar, supplies the book, and Leah Hager Cohen the lyrics. The BSO describes the piece as a response to Haydn's oratorio The Creation, which the orchestra performs the week before. The BSO says it explores the cosmos alongside human cruelty and tenderness, and asks what it means to answer the call to create.

The BSO compares its scale to Golijov's La Pasión según San Marcos, and WCRB reports that the music draws on jazz, Arabic and Brazilian traditions.

The performers

Eric Jacobsen, in his BSO conducting debut, leads the orchestra and the Tanglewood Festival Chorus. Vocalist Cécile McLorin Salvant and bass-baritone Davóne Tines are featured, joined by percussionist Cyro Baptista and Kayhan Kalhor on the kamancheh. The BSO x NEC Honors Children's Choir takes part, and the staging includes original artwork and animation by Alex Sopp and projection design by Camilla Tassi.

Dates, tickets

Performances are Thursday, Nov. 12 and Friday, Nov. 13 at 7:30 p.m., and Saturday, Nov. 14 at 8 p.m. Before the Saturday concert, at 6:30 p.m. in the Cabot-Cahners Room, Golijov speaks with the physicist and novelist Alan Lightman; this conversation is open to ticket holders for the 8 p.m. performance, and the BSO also asks for a separate RSVP because space is limited.

Symphony Hall is at 301 Massachusetts Avenue, Boston. Single tickets are sold through each performance's link on the BSO event page, which does not list individual prices. Phone 617-266-1200 or email customerservice@bso.org for help.

New music, a distinctive instrumental lineup and a question as old as Genesis make this one of the season's most talked-about nights in Boston.
