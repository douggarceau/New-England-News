Dover Quartet Brings Brahms and Dvorak to Jordan Hall, Nov. 15

Sunday, Nov. 15 at 3 p.m. at NEC's Jordan Hall in the Fenway, presented by Vivo Performing Arts with Brahms, Dvorak and Pura Fe.

Few chamber ensembles bring the sense of occasion that the Dover Quartet brings to a program built from three very different string quartets. On Sunday, Nov. 15, at 3 p.m., the group performs at NEC's Jordan Hall in the Fenway. Vivo Performing Arts presents the concert as an Aaron Richmond Recital, named for the organization's founder.

The program

The afternoon opens with Johannes Brahms's String Quartet No. 3 in B-flat major, Op. 67, written in 1875 and running about 34 minutes across four movements. Next comes Rattle Songs by Pura Fe, the Indigenous singer-songwriter, in an orchestration by the Chickasaw composer Jerod Impichchaachaaha' Tate. The concert closes with Antonin Dvorak's String Quartet No. 11 in C major, Op. 61, a warm and sweeping work of about 35 minutes.

The players

The quartet is made up of violinists Bryan Lee and Adam Barnett-Hart, violist Julianne Lee and cellist Camden Shaw. The event page notes that Barnett-Hart is replacing the listed first violinist, Joel Link, for this engagement. Barnett-Hart is a founding member of the Escher String Quartet, a former BBC New Generation Artist and a recipient of the Avery Fisher Career Grant, according to the page.

Getting there

Jordan Hall sits on the New England Conservatory campus. Tickets are sold online through the Vivo event page, which also lists 35 Under tickets through its app and member access. The page does not list ticket prices, so check the box office before you go.

The page praises the quartet's refined, luminous playing. Setting two late-nineteenth-century masterpieces beside a contemporary voice from the Indigenous tradition makes the afternoon feel like a conversation across generations, and it is a fine reason to spend a Sunday in the Fenway.
