Haydn's The Creation at Symphony Hall, Nov. 5-7

The Boston Symphony Orchestra and Philippe Jordan perform Haydn's oratorio Nov. 5, 6 and 7 at Symphony Hall on Massachusetts Avenue.

Joseph Haydn's oratorio The Creation, based on the Genesis story, returns to Boston: this week the Boston Symphony Orchestra brings it to Symphony Hall for the first time since 1998.

The work and the cast

Haydn composed The Creation late in his life for soloists, chorus and orchestra, drawing on the Genesis account of the world's making. The BSO places it at the opening of a two-part Creation festival that runs through Nov. 14, part of its season-long exploration of faith and spirituality in music.

Philippe Jordan conducts. Three soloists make their BSO debuts: soprano Louise Alder, tenor Bogdan Volkov and bass-baritone David Steffens. The Tanglewood Festival Chorus, prepared by Jean-Sébastien Vallée, the BSO's Director of Choruses, carries the great choral moments. The text is sung in German with English supertitles, so there is no need to know the language.

Dates and times

There are three performances at Symphony Hall: Thursday, Nov. 5 at 7:30 p.m.; Friday, Nov. 6 at 1:30 p.m.; and Saturday, Nov. 7 at 8 p.m.

Each night has a companion event, with its own separate ticket. On Thursday, Robert Hazen and Mike Wong give a 6:30 p.m. talk titled Time's Second Arrow in the Cabot-Cahners Room. On Friday, Teresa Neff offers a half-hour talk at 12:15 p.m. in the hall. On Saturday, Christian Wiman and Tracy K. Smith give a 6:30 p.m. reading in the Cabot-Cahners Room.

Tickets

Symphony Hall is at 301 Massachusetts Avenue, Boston. The BSO lists single-ticket links for each performance on its event page, though it does not publish single-ticket prices there. Subscription packages that include this concert start at $231 and run up to the thousands, depending on the series and seats. For help, call the BSO ticketing office at 617-266-1200 (Monday to Friday, 10 a.m. to 4 p.m.; Saturday, 12:30 to 4:30 p.m.) or email customerservice@bso.org.

The BSO's next orchestral concerts pair this oratorio with a brand-new work, the Golijov Creation.
