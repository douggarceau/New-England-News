Kapilow and A Far Cry Unpack Mozart's Clarinet Concerto, Nov. 22

Sunday, Nov. 22 at 3:00 PM at NEC's Jordan Hall in Fenway: Rob Kapilow, A Far Cry and clarinetist Romie de Guise-Langlois take on Mozart's Clarinet Concerto.

Rob Kapilow's What Makes It Great? series returns to Jordan Hall on Sunday, November 22, at 3:00 PM, presented by Vivo Performing Arts. This installment takes up Mozart's Clarinet Concerto, a late work, with A Far Cry and clarinetist Romie de Guise-Langlois joining Kapilow on stage.

The series is built on close listening. Kapilow, a music educator, combines detailed analysis with storytelling and humor, and the afternoon pairs an in-depth look at the concerto with a performance of it.

Who is performing

A Far Cry, which the organization describes as Boston's A Far Cry chamber orchestra, presents concerts, touring, recordings and education programs. Romie de Guise-Langlois is the clarinet soloist.

Jordan Hall, home of the New England Conservatory, is one of the city's principal chamber music halls, and the listing gives a 3:00 PM start.

Where and how to buy

The concert takes place at NEC's Jordan Hall, 290 Huntington Avenue, Boston. The event lists several price levels, and tickets are available through the Get Tickets button on the Vivo page. Questions about programming or ticketing can go to 617-482-6661 or boxoffice@vivoperformingarts.org.

Mozart wrote the Clarinet Concerto in 1791, the last year of his life, and it has become one of the most loved works in the clarinet repertoire. For listeners who know the piece well, a guided hearing can bring out details that a concert performance alone may pass over.

For listeners who want to know what they are hearing, the afternoon offers a concerto explained before it is played in full.
