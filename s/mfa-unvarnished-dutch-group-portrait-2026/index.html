Unvarnished at the MFA: Conservators Restore a Dutch Group Portrait

Opens Dec. 9 in the William A. Coolidge Gallery at the Museum of Fine Arts, 465 Huntington Avenue in the Fenway, and runs through Feb. 14, 2028.

Visitors to the Museum of Fine Arts usually see paintings only after their work is finished. Starting Dec. 9, a new exhibition invites them to watch one come back to life. Unvarnished: Conserving a Dutch Group Portrait takes over the William A. Coolidge Gallery, which belongs to the Center for Netherlandish Art, and it runs until Feb. 14, 2028.

The painting

The subject is The Founders of the Amsterdam Wine Merchants Guild, an oil on canvas by Nicolaes Eliasz. Pickenoy, dated 1625. The museum acquired it by purchase with funds by exchange. The exhibition page says it is one of the few Dutch group portraits of an organization rather than a family that a museum outside the Netherlands owns.

What visitors will see

The museum describes a pop-up conservation studio. Conservators will study the work with scientific techniques, then remove grime, discolored varnish and darkened overpaint. They will revarnish the surface and retouch areas where paint has been lost. The page says that by the end of treatment the painting will look much closer to what the artist intended.

Visiting

Because the work is done in view, visitors see the decisions a conservator has to make: how much old varnish to lift, which overpaint to remove, and how far to go in retouching so the painting still reads as the artist's.

The gallery is at 465 Huntington Avenue. The exhibition page links to the museum's ticket and visit pages and does not list exhibition-specific admission terms, so check those before you go. The show is a rare chance to see the unglamorous craft that keeps old pictures readable for later generations, and to watch a conservator's decisions unfold over many months.
