Igor Levit Brings an All-Beethoven Recital to Symphony Hall

Sunday, Nov. 8 from 3 to 5 p.m. at Symphony Hall, 301 Massachusetts Ave., Back Bay.

Pianist Igor Levit makes his Symphony Hall recital debut on Sunday, November 8, with an all-Beethoven program that the presenter, Vivo Performing Arts, describes as a portrait of the composer's inner life. The recital runs from 3:00 to 5:00 p.m.

The program traces a path from the young Beethoven's confident swagger, through the despair that came with his encroaching deafness, to a moment of reprieve and the Waldstein Sonata. That sonata, written around 1803 and 1804 and dedicated to the patron Count Ferdinand Ernst von Waldstein, is one of the composer's most ambitious works for piano and a staple of the repertoire.

Levit has performed with the Boston Symphony Orchestra before, but this is his first recital in Symphony Hall under the Vivo banner. The listing frames the afternoon as a statement of faith in the power of art, and an all-Beethoven program lets listeners follow one composer's arc from beginning to end.

Getting Tickets

The listing does not spell out every work on the program, so check the event page for the complete set before you go. Single tickets went on sale in August, and Vivo's Applause Circle membership offers early access to tickets. Details, availability and the program are at https://www.vivoperformingarts.org/productions/igor-levit-2026

Symphony Hall is in Back Bay, a short walk from Symphony station on the Green Line. Arrive a little early to find your seat and enjoy the hall before the first note.
