Zander Conducts Bruckner's Fifth at Symphony Hall, Nov. 22

Benjamin Zander leads the Boston Philharmonic Orchestra in Schubert and Bruckner Sunday, Nov. 22 at 3 p.m., Symphony Hall, Back Bay.

Some concerts are worth planning around because of what is on the stage: a conductor who loves the music, and a symphony the orchestra has almost never played. On Sunday, Nov. 22, Benjamin Zander leads the Boston Philharmonic Orchestra in Schubert's Unfinished Symphony and Bruckner's Symphony No. 5.

What you will hear

Schubert's Symphony No. 8, the Unfinished, runs about 25 minutes. Zander counts it among his favorite works. No one knows why Schubert left it unfinished, since he lived six more years after writing it, and it remains one of the most beloved works in the repertoire.

Bruckner's Fifth, about 81 minutes long, is the main event. The orchestra describes it as a magnificent, heaven-storming symphony of overwhelming grandeur, and notes that the Philharmonic has played it only once in its nearly 50-year history. It is a rare chance to hear it live.

Why now

The Boston Philharmonic is presenting its final season at Symphony Hall, according to a WBUR fall concert guide.

Details

The concert begins at 3 p.m. on Sunday, Nov. 22. A guide to the music with Zander begins at 1:45 p.m. Symphony Hall is at 301 Massachusetts Avenue, Boston, MA 02115.

Tickets are sold through the orchestra's website at bostonphil.org and the BSO's ticketing page for the performance. Ticket prices are not listed on either page. For phone help with BSO ticketing, call 617-266-1200 (Monday to Friday, 10 a.m. to 4 p.m.; Saturday, 12:30 to 4:30 p.m.).

A beloved symphony and a rarely performed one make a fitting pairing.
