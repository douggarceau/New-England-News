MBTA Opens No-Fare Pilot for Riders 75 and Older Who Stopped Driving

Enrollment opened Oct. 13 for older residents who have given up the car keys; here is how to sign up.

Older Bostonians who have handed in their car keys can now ride the T without paying a fare. Enrollment in a new MBTA pilot program opened Oct. 13 for Massachusetts residents age 75 and older who no longer drive.

The program is a joint effort of the MBTA and the Registry of Motor Vehicles, and it covers the whole system: buses, subway, ferries and Commuter Rail. It is part of a broader package of fare-relief measures for riders announced over the summer.

Who qualifies

Applicants must be Massachusetts residents, at least 75, and must hold a Massachusetts ID card, the state ID that does not carry driving privileges. Anyone who does not have one yet can apply for it through the RMV first.

How to sign up

There are two ways in. The online application lets riders have the credential mailed or pick it up in person. Or go straight to the Charlie Service Center at 296 Washington St., between Milk and Water streets, close to State Street and Downtown Crossing stations. The center is fully accessible, and walk-in applicants get a temporary credential the same day, with the permanent one following by mail.

Worth knowing

The state has not announced an end date for the pilot. For families helping a parent or grandparent, the in-person route is the quickest way to walk out with a working pass.
