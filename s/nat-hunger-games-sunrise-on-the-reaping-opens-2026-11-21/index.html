Haymitch Gets His Origin Story as Hunger Games Returns to Theaters

Sunrise on the Reaping is in theaters, with trackers betting it opens bigger than any Hunger Games film in a decade.

Panem is open for business again. Lionsgate's The Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping reached theaters on Nov. 20, according to SlashFilm, and it heads into Thanksgiving weekend as the movie to beat.

The story

The prequel adapts Suzanne Collins' novel about Haymitch Abernathy, the boozy mentor from the original films, back when he was a teenager thrown into the 50th Hunger Games. Joseph Zada plays young Haymitch, with McKenna Grace as Maysilee Donner and Elle Fanning as Effie Trinket, per SlashFilm.

There is a wrinkle for longtime fans. TheWrap reports that Woody Harrelson, Jennifer Lawrence and Josh Hutcherson return in a framing device, with the older Haymitch telling his story to Katniss and Peeta. SlashFilm describes those appearances as fairly brief, so do not expect a full reunion.

Who made it

Francis Lawrence, who has steered the series since Catching Fire, directs again, according to both outlets. SlashFilm puts the production budget at around $200 million.

The expectations

Industry watchers think this one is big. TheWrap reported on Nov. 18 that projections start at an $80 million domestic opening, with theater sources hoping for more than $100 million, and that presales are the best in franchise history. SlashFilm cited Box Office Theory forecasting a range of $105 million to $140 million.

That would be a sharp rebound. The last prequel, The Ballad of Songbirds and Snakes, opened to $44.6 million in 2023, both outlets report. The last entry to clear $100 million on opening weekend was Mockingjay Part 2, at $102 million in 2015.

The reviews and the rivals

Critics are mostly on board. TheWrap noted a 72 percent Rotten Tomatoes score at the time of its report, describing reviews as generally positive.

The competition is a mixed bag. SlashFilm lists the comedy Focker-In-Law and Disney's animated Hexed among the holiday releases. TheWrap argues the bigger draw on attention this weekend might be a video game, Grand Theft Auto VI, though it notes the two audiences do not overlap much.

Why it matters

If the forecasts hold, the series that helped define the 2010s young-adult boom proves it still has a crowd, and Lionsgate gets a reason to keep mining Collins' world. Either way, the film is in theaters now.
