Handel and Haydn's Messiah at Symphony Hall, Nov. 27-29

Harry Christophers leads the Handel and Haydn Society in its 173rd annual Messiah Nov. 27, 28 and 29 at Symphony Hall, Boston.

Boston keeps few traditions as faithfully as this one. The Handel and Haydn Society, founded in 1815, presents Handel's Messiah for the 173rd consecutive year over Thanksgiving weekend, at Symphony Hall.

The performance

The society's own account sets the weekend in context: this is its holiday tradition, and the 173rd consecutive year it has presented the piece. The Saturday matinee is the family-friendly choice, while Friday evening and Sunday afternoon suit those who prefer a different pace. The program runs nearly three hours with intermission, so plan the day around it, and arrive early to find your seat at Symphony Hall.

Harry Christophers, the society's Conductor Laureate, leads the H+H Orchestra and Chorus. The soloists are soprano Hilary Cronin, countertenor Christopher Lowrey, tenor Robert Murray and bass-baritone Roderick Williams. English supertitles accompany the sung text. The running time is about 2 hours 50 minutes, including intermission.

H+H describes the work as a holiday tradition.

Dates and times

Friday, Nov. 27 at 7:30 p.m.; Saturday, Nov. 28 at 3 p.m., which H+H designates a Boston Family Days performance; and Sunday, Nov. 29 at 3 p.m.

A pre-concert lecture, Musically Speaking, is offered, and the society links a Beyond Messiah history page on its website. A video conversation with Christophers and H+H President and CEO Lecolion Washington, Jr. takes place Tuesday, Nov. 17 at 1 p.m. and requires registration.

Tickets and venue

Symphony Hall is at 301 Massachusetts Avenue, Boston, MA 02115. Tickets are available at cart.handelandhaydn.org/overview/2109, or through the box office at 617-262-1815 or boxoffice@handelandhaydn.org. Phone hours are 10 a.m. to 4 p.m., and in-person hours are 9 a.m. to 5 p.m. The society does not list ticket prices on the Messiah page.

For a choral concert with deep Boston roots, this is the one.
