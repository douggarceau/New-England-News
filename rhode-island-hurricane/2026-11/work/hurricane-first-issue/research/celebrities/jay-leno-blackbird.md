# Jay Leno Meets the $2.5 Million Stick Shift


Hennessey’s Blackbird preview puts an extravagant price on a familiar pleasure: changing your own gears.

The man with the Newport mansion once admitted that he rented a car when he came to town. Jay Leno told Forbes in 2021 that the salt air was too hard on automobiles to keep his collection at Seafair. The house even had a six-car garage. A famous car collector still has to think about rust.[^1]

That practical streak makes an amusing companion to the machine getting a walkaround on Jay Leno’s Garage: Hennessey’s proposed Blackbird, with designer Nathan Malinick there to explain it. That is the pleasure of a walkaround: a chance to ask what the designer was thinking before anyone reaches for the keys.[^3]

Hennessey plans 71 cars, starting at $2.5 million before taxes, with customer production targeted for 2029–2030. Its August announcement describes a six-speed manual, rear-wheel drive and an interior with physical controls and no screens. The design takes its name and inspiration from the SR-71 Blackbird aircraft.[^2]

The proposed 6.2-liter V8, developed with Ilmor, has no turbochargers. Hennessey is targeting 800–850 horsepower, more than 9,000 rpm and a weight below 3,000 pounds. Its projected 2.5-second run to 60 mph and 220-mph top speed belong to the development brief; they are not independently established results for a delivered production car.[^2]

The promise also includes room for two carry-on bags and four cup holders. That is a wonderfully ordinary detail to find inside such an expensive fantasy. Someone, somewhere, has considered what happens to your coffee.[^2]

The interesting question is what this buys the person holding the wheel. A manual gearbox gives the driver another job: choosing a gear and getting the change right. An awkward change belongs to you. So does a good one. There is pleasure in learning a machine well enough to stop thinking about every movement. Paying millions for that pleasure is a different matter, but the pleasure itself is easy to understand.

Leno offered his own explanation of expensive enthusiasms in that Forbes interview. He and Mavis had bought Seafair in 2017 for $13.5 million after spotting it during a visit to Newport. He described asking to buy the place furnished, right down to the ketchup in the refrigerator. The appeal, he said, was having a house he liked, ready to use.[^1]

“I don’t buy things for investment. I buy things because I like them,” he told Forbes.[^1]

Production is years away. The useful questions will be how it shifts, how it rides and whether the owner wants to take the long way home. A specification sheet cannot settle those.

Meanwhile, anyone tempted by the Blackbird’s sales pitch can try the inexpensive part first: find a manual car, put away the phone and enjoy the drive. The $2.5 million can wait.
