# The Part of Vinny Paz’s Movie That Made Him Cry


Ten years after Bleed for This reached theaters, Rhode Island’s comeback story has a Hall of Fame ending—and a much more personal heart.

Vinny Paz knew what a broken neck felt like. He knew the pain of having a medical halo removed from his skull. But when Hollywood put his life on screen, the sight that made him cry was his mother and father.[^1]

“It made me tear up that they couldn’t see it,” he told the Associated Press in November 2016. Both parents had died before Bleed for This opened. Katey Sagal played his mother, Louise; Ciarán Hinds played his father, Angelo.[^1]

November 18 marks ten years since the film’s theatrical release. Miles Teller played Paz, and Ben Younger directed the production, filmed entirely in Rhode Island. For the fighter who grew up in Cranston, the family resemblance mattered: he told AP that the movie caught the tough love of his working-class home.[^1]

The boxing record supplies plenty of material before anyone gets to the crash. Paz won the IBF lightweight title against Greg Haugen in Providence in June 1987. Haugen took it back in their next meeting; Paz won the third fight in 1990. A three-fight argument is a fairly good introduction to this career.[^2]

In October 1991, he stopped Gilbert Delé in the 12th round in Providence to win the WBA junior middleweight championship. Soon afterward, a car crash broke his neck. The movie’s extraordinary premise comes from what happened next: a champion had to work his way back to being a boxer at all.[^2]

Paz’s official biography describes him training with the halo still attached, against medical orders. It records his return against Luis Santana more than a year after the accident, winning a ten-round decision.[^2]

The comeback also had a long afterlife. His published fight record lists wins over Roberto Durán in 1994 and 1995, a loss to Roy Jones Jr. later in 1995, and a final victory over Tocker Pudwill in 2004. He finished with 50 wins, 10 losses and 30 knockouts. The losses belong in the story, too. Returning did not make him unbeatable. It gave him a career to keep fighting through.[^3][^5]

Then came a distinction the film could not have included: induction into the International Boxing Hall of Fame in 2025. The World Boxing Council’s account places Paz among the inductees honored in Canastota, New York, on June 8, alongside Manny Pacquiao and the rest of that year’s class.[^4][^5]

The Hall’s induction program describes his long wait for that recognition. When the selection was announced in December 2024, the fighter’s response, published in the Hall’s news release, needed very little interpretation: “This is the best phone call I’ve ever taken!” The Pazmanian Devil was delighted.[^6][^7]

For anyone meeting the story now, the film offers a way in. As of October 23, Paramount+ listed Bleed for This for its Premium plan, with a running time of one hour and 56 minutes and an R rating. Check the current listing before making movie-night plans.[^8]

After everything it took to get his life into a movie, Paz still wanted his parents there to see it.[^1]
