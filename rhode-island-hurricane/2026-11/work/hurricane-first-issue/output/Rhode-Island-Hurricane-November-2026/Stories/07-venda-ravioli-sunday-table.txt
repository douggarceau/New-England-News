SUNDAY STARTS AT VENDA

Ravioli, pumpkin tortelloni and the Costantino family’s long life on Federal Hill.

Somebody has to make the ravioli. At Venda, that job has supported a Federal Hill business for generations and given the rest of us a head start on Sunday dinner.

Venda dates its pasta business to 1938. Alan Costantino bought it in 1972, and the company says he remains involved alongside his children. The market expanded into its present home on DePasquale Square in 2001. That is a substantial span of dinners, holidays and changing households for one pasta business to have accompanied.

The current pickup menu includes five-cheese and spinach ravioli, plus prosciutto-and-provolone ravioli. For an autumn dinner, there is pumpkin tortelloni. Picking a filling may turn out to be the difficult part.

“It all starts with the ingredients,” Costantino told Food Finessa in 2022. He pointed to flour and cheeses, including ricotta and pecorino paglierino. The interview returned a growing business to the contents of the pasta itself.

Venda’s own account now lists more than 150 varieties of fresh and frozen pasta. Across the square, the family’s restaurant offers another way to gather. Together, the businesses accommodate different amounts of time, energy and ambition: the meal you assemble at home, or the one somebody else serves.

The company’s catalog stretches well past ravioli, through manicotti, stuffed shells, tortellini and angel hair. That is plenty of room to change dinner without giving up the Sunday ritual.
