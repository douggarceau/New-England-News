CHOWDER WEATHER

How a father’s nickname and a son’s promise became a Rhode Island clam-shack fixture.

November is a perfectly reasonable month to think about clam cakes. The beach towel may be put away; dinner still needs deciding. At Oakland Beach in Warwick, Iggy’s Doughboys & Chowder House keeps the pairing of chowder and clam cakes on the menu through the year.

The business began in 1989 with Gaetano and Sally Gravino. Gaetano was a hairstylist who had taken extra work at the beach stand before the couple had the chance to lease it. Even the name came from home: the family’s history traces Iggy to a nickname his son David made from the letters on Gaetano’s license plate.

Rhode Island Monthly traced the early chowder specialty to Sally’s side of the family: Manhattan red. A restaurant now known for offering choices started with a particular family recipe.

Today, the Warwick menu offers three chowders: New England white, Manhattan red and Rhode Island clear. It also lists stuffed quahogs, whole-belly clams, calamari and fish and chips. The chowder-and-clam-cake combinations keep the original relationship easy to find amid the larger selection. Doughboys, the fried-dough sweet in the business’s name, occupy their own part of the menu.

The original Warwick location operates year-round, according to its official page. The clam cakes do not disappear with the beach towels. Neither does the argument over which chowder belongs beside them.

The family’s history records a more serious promise behind the business. Before Gaetano died, he asked David to carry it on. David was a senior at Johnson & Wales, with choices ahead of him. He kept the promise.
