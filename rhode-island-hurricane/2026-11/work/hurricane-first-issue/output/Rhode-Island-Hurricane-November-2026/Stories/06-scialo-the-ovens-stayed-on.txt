THE OVENS STAYED ON

Scialo Bros. nearly became a Federal Hill memory. Keeping its Italian baking tradition alive took working ovens, old recipes and a new beginning.

When Scialo Bros. Bakery closed in 2020, the ovens kept going. In his report on the shop’s return the following year, WJAR’s R.J. Heim explained why: the old brick structures had been kept heated because there was concern that cooling them could cause damage. Even with the door shut, this bakery required care.

The shop had opened on Providence’s Federal Hill in 1916. Sisters Carol Scialo Gaeta and Lois Scialo Ellis later carried on their father Luigi’s business. By the time investors arranged its 2021 reopening, preserving the name was only part of the deal. The family recipes and working ovens mattered, too. WJAR reported that Carol returned as an employee, bringing her knowledge into the next chapter.

The job came with a change of status, from owner to employee, but Gaeta sounded pleased to be back. “Retirement is no fun!” she told WJAR in 2021.

The current menu at 257 Atwells Avenue includes ricotta pie, blackberry crostata, wine biscuits and cannoli. The fall selection adds an apricot-and-almond-paste tart. Somebody still has to settle the argument over what to bring to dinner.

There are new hands in the story. In its October 12, 2026 feature, Providence Business News identified general manager Olivia DeAngelis as the bakery’s public face since the reopening. The business continues beyond the original family’s ownership, with the old name still above the work.

In 2026, the Scialo name is 110 years old. The bakery has changed hands and survived a closure. There are still cannoli on Atwells Avenue bearing that name.
