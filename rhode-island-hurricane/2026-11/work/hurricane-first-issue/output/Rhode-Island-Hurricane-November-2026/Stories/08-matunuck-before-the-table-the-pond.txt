THE FARMER BEHIND MATUNUCK OYSTER BAR

Perry Raso needed a dock for his oyster boats. The restaurant came next. Then a fire tested everything he had built.

Perry Raso’s restaurant story starts underwater. He founded Matunuck Oyster Farm on Potter Pond in South Kingstown in 2002, seven years before opening Matunuck Oyster Bar. The business began with growing shellfish and finding a way to get the work done.

Raso had started digging littlenecks in Point Judith Pond at twelve, according to the company’s biography, and went on to study aquaculture and fisheries technology at the University of Rhode Island. In a July 2026 interview with Ocean State Media’s Ian Donnis, he recalled the lean first years of the oyster farm, when harvesting other shellfish helped bring in money while the oysters grew.

The restaurant followed a practical need. Raso wanted commercial dock access for his oyster boats. He bought the waterside property and opened the restaurant in 2009. The boats needed a place to land; the restaurant gave the farm another way to reach customers.

Then came the fire in May 2025. In Donnis’s July 9, 2026 report, Raso was serving customers under a temporary tent while rebuilding the original restaurant. He was looking toward a fall reopening. It was an account of a business still in recovery, with the next stage unfinished.

The farm’s published tour description traces aquaculture and shellfish through different stages of growth. Matunuck’s own oysters are raised in Potter Pond. That connection between a specific pond and the plate was there before the fire, before the tent and before there was a restaurant.

Raso told Donnis that he initially resisted a suggestion to put a tent across the street, worrying it would damage the business’s reputation. He changed his mind. “It really saved us,” he said.
